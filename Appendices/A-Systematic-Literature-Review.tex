\chapter{Systematic Literature Review}
\label{app:systematic-literature-review}

This appendix documents the literature-review procedure, the searches
executed on 14 October 2026 and the full-text assessments supporting
Chapter~\ref{cha:state-of-the-art}. The detailed method belongs here so that
the main chapter can concentrate on the comparison and its implications.
The searches have been exported, but selection from the complete corpus
has not been finished. Thirteen studies have been assessed for the current
synthesis. They are not presented as the final inclusion set.

A systematic review makes the route from a research question to the
selected evidence inspectable. Its protocol specifies the questions,
searches, eligibility criteria, quality assessment and synthesis procedure
\parencite[Section~5.4]{kitchenham2007guidelines}. PRISMA-S informs the
reporting of search sources, exact strategies, dates and duplicate removal
\parencite{rethlefsen2021prismas}. Neither citation establishes that an
unfinished review meets every reporting requirement.

\section{Review Questions and Boundaries}

The literature questions support the thesis research questions introduced
in Section~\ref{sec:introduction:research-questions}.

\begin{description}
\item[LQ1:] How do existing approaches connect telemetry across components
and identify faults, including when shared identifiers or records are missing?
\item[LQ2:] Which security-related fields and derived features have been
used for diagnosis, and how has their relevance been assessed?
\item[LQ3:] How do LLM-based agents investigate operational failures, and
how are diagnostic accuracy, consistency and supporting evidence evaluated?
\item[LQ4:] How do remediation approaches restrict and approve changes,
and what evidence is reported about validation, recovery and rollback?
\end{description}

The technical scope covers cloud services, distributed applications and
the network or security controls supporting them. A relevant study need
not use Cloudflare, Azure or multiple agents. Non-agent approaches provide
important alternatives for correlation and diagnosis. The publication
window begins on 1 January 2020 and ends on 14 October 2026. Older review
methodology, standards and product documentation are contextual sources,
not technical studies included under this date criterion.

\section{Information Sources and Executed Searches}

IEEE Xplore and OpenAlex were the two main sources. No ACM query was
successfully executed. OpenAlex is an index covering multiple publishers
and repositories, not an equivalent replacement for an ACM Digital Library
search. An indexed record does not by itself establish peer review.

The OpenAlex Works API used \path{search.title} for the topic expression
and \path{search.title_and_abstract} for context. Publication-date filters
covered the exact window above. Cursor pagination retained all returned
records. The database documents field-specific and Boolean searches
\parencite{openalex2026search}.

IEEE used \enquote{Document Title} for each topic term and
\enquote{All Metadata} for contextual terms, with a 2020--2026 year filter.
Exact publication dates still require checking during selection because
a year filter cannot enforce the October cut-off on its own. Citation
exports with abstracts were retained for S1--S3. For S4, the complete
45-result title and document-ID list was captured from the visible results.
The attempted citation download did not yield a retained file. S4's retained
metadata are therefore less complete and require enrichment during screening.

Each expression below has a title block T and a context block C. OpenAlex
applied T AND C in the fields specified above. IEEE applied the same Boolean
structure with field prefixes on individual terms. The machine-readable
expressions, page request URLs, export hashes and metadata records are in
\texttt{Review/2026-10-14/}. Search order is not an eligibility criterion.

\section{Eligibility, Deduplication and Selection}

A study is eligible when it is an English-language primary study in the
date window, addresses at least one literature question and reports a
relevant empirical evaluation. Operational records, simulations and
controlled testbeds can qualify. Their differences must be retained in the
assessment. Reviews, conceptual proposals and product descriptions may
provide context but do not independently establish effectiveness.

An intrusion classifier does not qualify solely because it uses network
data. Its analysis must contribute relevant information about telemetry,
features or investigation. Uncertain records require full-text assessment.
Negative findings are not an exclusion reason.

The bulk full-text retrieval log contains 542 processed records: 37 PDF
payloads, 25 records yielding only HTML and 480 unsuccessful records. One
PDF payload is corrupt. These figures describe retrieval attempts, not
eligibility. HTML can be only a landing page or an access challenge, and a
successfully downloaded PDF still requires assessment. The bulk process
was interrupted by a network-policy restriction. Such errors do not prove
that the papers cannot be obtained through institutional access. Unattempted
records are not classified as unavailable. Previously retained author
copies and web-accessible full texts supplemented this process.

The planned single round of reference-list checking is also unfinished.
The current supplementary set comes from Background and earlier exploratory
work. It must not be described as a completed systematic snowballing round.
Neither university-authenticated retrieval nor independent human screening
is claimed.

\section{Quality Assessment and Extraction}
\label{sec:slr:quality}

The six appraisal questions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Are the problem, system and relevant assumptions described?
\item Are the data sources, scenarios and reference answers explained?
\item Are the evaluation procedure and outcome measures clear?
\item Do the comparisons support the claimed improvement?
\item Are variation and material sources of bias addressed?
\item Is enough information available to inspect the method and its limits?
\end{enumerate}

Judgements are recorded separately as supported, partly supported, not
supported, not reported or not applicable. They are not added into a total
score. A weak comparison qualifies an improvement claim, while missing
reporting remains distinct from evidence that a procedure was not performed.
Quality concerns do not silently remove unfavourable studies.

Each assessment records the source version, relevant questions, data and
evaluation setting, supported finding, source locator and limitations.
Project implications are separately identified as reviewer inferences.
\texttt{Review/study-extraction.json} contains the assessments. The
summaries below accompany the main comparison rather than duplicating all
reported numerical results.

The assistant performed these assessments. The author has not yet verified
them. The previously proposed blinded recheck by the thesis author has not
been performed, and no inter-reviewer agreement statistic is reported.
Checking citations and arithmetic during writing is not an independent
screening or extraction exercise.

\section{Remediation Controls: Evidence and Limits}

The most detailed remediation controls in the current evidence set are
reported by STRATUS. Its controls must be distinguished by what is
implemented, experimentally examined or only supported as a capability.
Table~\ref{tab:slr:controls} avoids treating these categories as equivalent.

\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.25\textwidth}X@{}}
\toprule

Dry run & Described for applicable commands. The effectiveness of dry runs is not isolated by the reported ablation. \\

\end{tabularx}

For the thesis, allow-list enforcement, approval, execution, recovery
verification and rollback should therefore have separate observations.
A dry run cannot establish that a live change will restore the endpoint.
An approval cannot establish that the proposed diagnosis is correct. These
are evaluation distinctions derived from the comparison, not claims that
the PoC has implemented or validated those controls.

\section{Synthesis Procedure and Implications}

The synthesis is thematic. It compares assumptions, tasks, evidence and
limitations within each literature question. No pooled effect size or
cross-paper accuracy league table is calculated. The studies evaluate
incompatible units, including metric rankings, reconstructed traces,
incident categories, generated explanations and live repairs. Even a
shared term such as accuracy can refer to a different denominator.

The comparison supports five concrete priorities for the PoC: preserve
provenance when linking observations, validate the meaning of security
features, constrain the tools exposed to agents, separate diagnostic
correctness from explanation quality, and verify repairs independently of
command success. These priorities follow from the assessed evidence. The
particular tools, thresholds, baselines and number of experimental runs
remain design decisions to justify in the methodology and implementation.

Implementation observations can also refine the review. For example, the
reported shared application trace identifier provides a concrete starting
point for testing explicit correlation. It does not show that the same
identifier crosses Cloudflare and firewall logs. Inspecting those sources
can reveal a need to investigate time-based joins, topology evidence or
missing-data handling. Any new search arising from that inspection should
receive its actual date, expression and rationale. The existing history
should not be rewritten to make later decisions appear pre-planned.

\section{Limitations and Work Required for Completion}

The dominant limitation is unfinished selection. The thirteen assessments
are useful evidence, but cannot establish the final balance of findings
across the 954-record corpus. Prior familiarity and easier access influenced
which texts could be assessed first. An exhaustive research-gap claim would
therefore be premature.

Search coverage is also limited by title-focused terminology, different
fields across sources, the English-language boundary and the 2020 cut-off.
The observed misses of relevant seed papers are a concrete reason to
reconsider recall. OpenAlex publication metadata and preprint versions
require checking, while the IEEE S4 export lacks abstracts. Replacing ACM
because of access difficulties does not remove these source differences.

Three assessed sources are preprints. Their findings are identified as such
and tied to the versions examined. Publication does not by itself guarantee
strong evidence, but peer-review status remains useful context. Private
industrial datasets, small controlled fault sets, reused benchmarks and
LLM-based evaluation further limit transfer to the Norlys path.

Completion requires resolving the outstanding record-level decisions,
obtaining and assessing eligible full texts where access permits, linking
related versions, finishing the planned reference-list round and verifying
the extractions. Final counts must then reconcile identification, duplicate
removal, exclusions, unavailable reports and included studies. The synthesis
must be revised if additional studies change its conclusions. Until these
steps are complete, this appendix is a transparent review report with a
substantive evidence synthesis, not a claim of a completed SLR.
