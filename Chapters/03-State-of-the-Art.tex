\chapter{State of the Art}
\label{cha:state-of-the-art}

This chapter examines research relevant to investigating failures across
the Cloudflare-to-Azure request path. The review connects four topics:
telemetry correlation, security-relevant data, agent-based diagnosis and
controlled remediation. Its purpose is to compare existing approaches and
the evidence supporting them. This comparison will inform the design and
evaluation of the proof of concept within the focused Norlys scope
\parencite[Scope]{norlys_operations_agents}.

\section{Systematic Literature Review Method}
\label{sec:state-of-the-art:review-method}

A systematic literature review uses an explicit procedure to find, select
and assess research. Defining that procedure before selecting studies
helps reduce the risk of choosing only evidence that supports an expected
conclusion. Kitchenham and Charters describe a review protocol covering
the questions, search strategy, selection criteria, quality assessment,
data extraction and synthesis
\parencite[Section~5.4]{kitchenham2007guidelines}.

The following protocol proposes these decisions for this thesis.
The papers cited in Background provide possible starting points. They
have not yet been selected through this review procedure.

\subsection{Review Questions}

The review questions concern evidence from existing studies. They support
the four research questions in
Section~\ref{sec:introduction:research-questions}, which concern the system
developed and evaluated in this thesis.

\begin{description}
    \item[LQ1:] How do existing approaches connect telemetry across
    components to reconstruct failing requests and identify faults,
    including when shared identifiers or records are missing?
    This question supports RQ1.

    \item[LQ2:] Which security-related telemetry fields and derived features
    have been used for diagnosis, and how has their relevance been assessed?
    This question supports RQ2.

    \item[LQ3:] How do LLM-based agents investigate operational failures,
    and how are diagnostic accuracy, consistency and supporting evidence
    evaluated? This question supports RQ3 and the agent coordination
    aspect of RQ1.

    \item[LQ4:] How do operational remediation approaches restrict and
    approve changes, and what evidence is reported about validation,
    recovery and rollback? This question supports RQ4.
\end{description}

The review will consider studies of cloud services, distributed
applications and the network or security controls supporting them. A
study need not use Cloudflare or Azure to be relevant. Its assumptions
and evaluation setting must, however, be examined before applying its
findings to the selected request path. Non-agent approaches will also be
considered where they address correlation, diagnosis or remediation.
Requiring every paper to use multiple agents would prevent a useful
comparison with alternative approaches.

\subsection{Information Sources and Search Strategy}

The proposed main sources are IEEE Xplore, the ACM Digital Library and
Scopus. Together, these provide a publisher-based search and a broader
index search. The proposed publication period starts on 1 January 2020
and ends on the recorded final search date. This boundary focuses the
review on recent technical work. Earlier methodological sources, such as
the software engineering review guidelines, are used to design the
review and are outside the set of technical studies being compared.

Four search families will address the review questions separately.
Each will combine a setting, such as cloud services or network security,
with the relevant task, such as correlation, diagnosis or remediation.
For example, an initial query for operational agents is:

\begin{quote}
\small\raggedright
(\enquote{large language model} OR LLM OR \enquote{LLM agent}
OR \enquote{multi-agent}) AND
(cloud OR microservice OR microservices OR AIOps
OR \enquote{distributed system} OR \enquote{distributed systems}) AND
(diagnosis OR diagnostic OR troubleshooting OR \enquote{root cause}
OR \enquote{incident management})
\end{quote}

The four initial queries are recorded in the accompanying review records.
They will be adapted to each database's search syntax, using titles,
abstracts and keywords where supported. Search fields and any differences
between interfaces will be recorded explicitly. A pilot will check the
volume and relevance of results and whether known, relevant papers can
be retrieved. Finding those papers is a useful check, but does not
demonstrate that a search is complete.

The proposed supplementary search has two parts. First, the references
and citing papers of eligible database results and relevant Background
papers will be checked for one round. Newly found papers will be screened
using the same criteria. Their references will not be followed recursively
unless a protocol amendment is recorded. Second, the agent and remediation
searches will be adapted for arXiv. Eligible preprints will be identified
separately from peer-reviewed studies. Surveys can help locate primary
studies, but their summaries will not be counted as independent evidence
of a system's effectiveness.

For every search, the record will contain the source, exact query, search
fields, filters, date, returned count and retained export. PRISMA-S provides
reporting guidance for documenting these details and the removal of
duplicate records \parencite{rethlefsen2021prismas}. It is used here to
support transparent reporting, rather than as a claim that the review has
already met a reporting checklist. A general web search will not be
reported as a search performed inside a named academic database.

\subsection{Eligibility and Study Selection}

The proposed review includes English-language primary studies within the
date range that address at least one review question. A study must
describe an approach and provide an empirical evaluation relevant to
operational investigation, diagnostic data or remediation. Evaluations
may use operational records, controlled experiments or testbeds. The
setting and its limitations will be recorded.

Studies will be excluded from the main comparison if they address an
unrelated domain, describe only an unevaluated proposal, or concern
telemetry storage and collection without a relevant diagnostic task.
Intrusion-detection studies will qualify only when they examine telemetry
or features relevant to understanding network or application security
events. A classifier benchmark without such analysis will not qualify
solely because it uses security data. Studies of general agent frameworks
or human decision-making outside operations may still support Background,
but will not be presented as operational evaluations. Product documentation,
standards and the Norlys specifications remain contextual sources.

Records will first be checked for duplicates using their DOI, title,
authors and publication details. Different reports of the same study,
including preprint and published versions, will be linked under one study
identifier. The published version will normally provide the main account.
Additional results in a related report can be retained without counting
the underlying study twice.

Screening will then proceed from titles and abstracts to full texts.
Uncertain cases will move to full-text assessment. Each full-text
exclusion will have a recorded reason. If a full text cannot be obtained
through AU access or a lawful author copy, it will be recorded as not
retrieved rather than rejected for its findings. The review will report
separate counts for retrieved records, duplicates, screened records,
unavailable reports and included studies.

The proposed procedure uses the thesis author as the reviewer. Before
formal screening, up to five records from each search family and one
available full text per family will be used to test the criteria and
extraction form. These are calibration exercises, not a limit on the
number of included papers. After screening, a random ten per cent of
decisions, with at least ten where available, will be reassessed without
displaying the original decisions. This check will also be applied to
extraction records. Differences and their resolution will be retained.
It does not replace independent review by a second person. Any AI
assistance will be documented, and proposed decisions and extracted
claims will require the author's verification against the papers.

\subsection{Quality Assessment and Information Extraction}

Each included study will be assessed against six questions developed for
this review:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item Are the problem, system and relevant assumptions described?
    \item Are the data sources, scenarios and reference answers explained?
    \item Are the evaluation procedure and outcome measures clear?
    \item Do the comparisons support the claimed improvement, where an
    improvement is claimed?
    \item Are repeated trials, variation and possible sources of bias
    addressed where relevant?
    \item Is enough information available to inspect the method and
    understand its limitations?
\end{enumerate}

Each judgement will be recorded as supported, partly supported, not
supported, not reported or not applicable, with a source reference and a short
reason. The judgements will remain separate rather than being combined
into a single score. Missing reporting will not be treated as proof that
a procedure was absent. Quality concerns will qualify the conclusions
drawn from a study rather than silently removing unfavourable results.

The extraction form will record the study's setting, telemetry sources,
correlation method, security fields and derived features. For agent
systems, it will also record roles, tools, coordination, model information
and how conclusions are connected to evidence. Remediation fields will
cover permitted actions, access restrictions, approval, dry runs, recovery
checks, audit records and rollback. Each control will be distinguished as
proposed, implemented, evaluated, explicitly absent or not reported.

Evaluation records will retain scenario counts, ground truth, baselines,
metric definitions, reported results, repeated runs, time and cost where
available. Claims about diagnostic evidence and remediation success will
be linked to the paper's actual assessment procedure. Every extracted
finding will have a source location. Unavailable values will remain
marked as not reported.

\subsection{Comparison and Interpretation}

The proposed synthesis will group studies by the four review topics and
compare their approaches in a common evidence table. A study may inform
several topics while retaining one study identifier. The discussion will
consider which assumptions each approach requires, what was tested and
how directly the results apply to the thesis setting.

Reported percentages will not be ranked as if they measured the same
task. For example, selecting a faulty service and explaining an incorrect
firewall rule require different levels of diagnostic detail. The initial
plan is therefore a structured narrative comparison rather than a pooled
statistical estimate. The comparison will also check whether its main
conclusions change when preprints or studies with substantial quality
concerns are considered separately.

Possible research gaps will be identified only after this comparison.
They will be stated within the limits of the searched literature. A
capability that is not reported is different from one that was tested
and found ineffective. Likewise, the absence of a paper using the exact
Norlys product combination is not, by itself, a research contribution.
The review's limitations will include its date and language boundaries,
source coverage and reliance on one reviewer. Applying evidence from
large or synthetic workloads to an environment with limited operational
traffic will require particular attention.
